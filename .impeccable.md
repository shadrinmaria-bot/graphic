# Impeccable

## Design Context

### Users
Design studios, agencies and art directors reviewing Maria for a role. They
arrive from a link, skim fast, and judge craft before they read a word. The job
to be done is: see the work, believe the hand behind it, and find a way to make
contact without hunting. A commissioning client may wander in, but the site is
tuned for the hiring reader first.

Secondary constraint: the work is bilingual. Three of five projects carry
Hebrew titles and two carry Hebrew descriptions, so the layout has to hold
right-to-left text inside a left-to-right page without looking accidental.

### Brand Personality
Pressed, patient, exact. The hero is a pressed-flower composition: real plant
material arranged with tweezers and photographed flat. Everything else should
feel like it was placed by the same hand. Warm rather than clinical, quiet
rather than loud, and precise rather than casual. The site should read as
restrained on purpose, never as unfinished.

### Aesthetic Direction
Warm neutral ground (#F8F6F4), mauve ink (#7A6565 primary, #806B6B for labels
and meta), dusty rose accent (#D5B4B4). The rose is decorative only: it carries
rules and hover states, never text, because at 1.77:1 it cannot be read.
Secondary text is a second ink tone, never the primary tone dimmed with opacity.
Editorial: hairline rules, generous ground, asymmetric placement, small-caps
labels. Inria Serif for display, with Frank Ruhl Libre picking up Hebrew;
Assistant for body and labels, which carries Latin and Hebrew in one family.
Squared corners throughout, no shadows, no cards.

One type scale, each step 1.25x the last: 12.8 / 16 / 20 / 25px, then three
fluid display steps. Seven sizes render across the whole site. A new size is a
last resort, not a convenience.

Anti-references: the SaaS template look, drop-shadowed rounded cards, gradient
text, glassmorphism, icon-above-heading grids, centred everything.

### Design Principles
1. The work is the loudest thing on the page. Nothing decorative may outrank it.
2. Restraint has to look deliberate. Where the page is quiet, the typography
   and spacing must be exact enough that the quiet reads as a choice.
3. Asymmetry over centring. The pieces are placed off-axis on purpose; the
   supporting text should follow the same logic instead of retreating to centre.
4. Bilingual by construction, not by patch. Hebrew gets correct direction and
   correct alignment as a rule of the system, not a fix applied per element.
5. Motion is a signature, not decoration. The hero drifts because the artwork is
   made of loose objects. Everything else holds still.
